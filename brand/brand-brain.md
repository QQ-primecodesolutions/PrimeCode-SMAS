## 1. Brand Definition

PrimeCode Solutions is a small software development company focused on helping small businesses and organisations establish a stronger digital presence and begin their digital transformation journey.

PrimeCode initially focuses on **Phuthaditjhaba, Qwaqwa and the wider Free State**, with the long-term ambition of expanding beyond the region.

PrimeCode is not simply a website provider. Its broader purpose is to help businesses and organisations understand how technology can solve real problems, improve how they operate, and present themselves professionally in the digital world.

### Core positioning

> **PrimeCode Solutions helps businesses turn ideas and real-world problems into practical digital solutions.**

### Geographic mission

> **Bring meaningful digital transformation to our own township and community first, then take that capability further.**

---

## 2. Brand Vision

### Vision

To contribute to a digitally empowered Qwaqwa and South Africa by making useful, professional and accessible technology available to businesses and organisations that may otherwise struggle to access it.

### Mission

To help small businesses, entrepreneurs, schools, NGOs and organisations establish a professional digital presence and use technology to solve practical problems.

### Long-term ambition

PrimeCode should eventually evolve from primarily providing websites and software development into a broader technology partner capable of delivering:

- Web applications
- Mobile applications
- Custom software
- Backend systems
- APIs
- System integrations
- Automation
- UI/UX
- IT support
- Digital transformation consulting
- SEO
- Other technology solutions

Future capabilities should not be presented as current commercial services until PrimeCode is ready to actively offer them.

---

## 3. PrimeCode Promise

PrimeCode should consistently communicate four things:

1. **We understand your problem.**  
   Technology should begin with customer needs, not with a technology stack.

2. **We build practical solutions.**  
   The solution should serve the business rather than introduce unnecessary complexity.

3. **We care about quality.**  
   Affordable should never mean cheap-looking or poor quality.

4. **We want to grow with our community.**  
   PrimeCode's local roots are part of its competitive advantage.

## 17. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1 — PrimeCode Explains
Technology education.

Examples:
- What is an API?
- What is a database?
- Website vs web application
- What is cloud hosting?
- What is HTTPS?
- What is authentication?
- What is responsive design?

**Purpose:** Build technical credibility.

### Pillar 2 — Tech for Business
Technology explained through business problems.

Examples:
- When WhatsApp isn't enough
- When spreadsheets become a problem
- Why your business needs a website
- How online booking can help
- When custom software makes sense
- Does your business need an app?

**Purpose:** Create demand.

### Pillar 3 — Behind the Code
Show the work.

Examples:
- Database design
- UI development
- API development
- Testing
- Deployment
- Git
- Architecture
- Debugging
- Development workflow

**Purpose:** Demonstrate capability.

### Pillar 4 — Build With PrimeCode
Show actual work.

Examples:
- Project Spotlights
- Website launches
- Case studies
- Before/after
- Project walkthroughs
- Features built

**Purpose:** Provide evidence.

### Pillar 5 — PrimeCode Insight
Opinions and observations.

Examples:
- Your business probably doesn't need an app
- A website isn't just a digital brochure
- Technology should solve problems, not create them
- Why small businesses should think about digital ownership

**Purpose:** Build authority and personality.

### Pillar 6 — The PrimeCode Journey
Founder and company story.

Examples:
- Why PrimeCode exists
- Lessons from building a software company
- What we're learning
- Development challenges
- Local technology opportunities
- Building technology in Qwaqwa

**Purpose:** Humanise the company.

## 22. Visual Identity

Create reusable PrimeCode templates:

| Code | Series | Design direction |
|---|---|---|
| **TECH-01** | PrimeCode Explains | Large question headline on dark background, simple diagram, logo bottom-right |
| **BIZ-01** | Tech for Business | Problem statement slide 1, solution slides after, tick/cross iconography |
| **CODE-01** | Behind the Code | Screenshot or diagram-led, monospace accents, minimal copy |
| **PROJECT-01** | Build With PrimeCode | Device mockup of the live site, problem/approach/result structure |
| **INSIGHT-01** | PrimeCode Insight | Typography only. The sentence is the design. |
| **BRAND-01** | PrimeCode Services | Service icon + short line, consistent grid, brand colours |
| **JOURNEY-01** | PrimeCode Journey | Real photograph, light text overlay, long caption does the work |
| **LOCAL-01** | Digital Transformation in Qwaqwa | "What if..." framing, local imagery, before/after concept frames |

These eight codes come from the content calendar workbook's Reference sheet and are canonical.
(v1.0 used `BUSINESS-01` and had `BRAND-01` = Journey.) A post's series and its template are
separate fields: PC-2026-001 is a Journey post rendered with the typographic `INSIGHT-01` design.

Brand colours, fonts and the logo are in `brand/tokens.ts` (the PrimeCode website's palette, Inter
and JetBrains Mono).

Consistency is more important than visual complexity.

## 26. AI Content Rules

AI-generated content must never:

- Invent clients
- Invent testimonials
- Invent statistics
- Invent project results
- Invent services
- Claim PrimeCode built something it did not build
- Claim expertise PrimeCode does not possess
- Create fake customer stories
- Create fake reviews
- Present speculation as fact

AI drafts require human approval before publication.

---

## 27. Approval Workflow

Every post follows:

**IDEA → DRAFT → AI QUALITY CHECK → HUMAN REVIEW → APPROVED → SCHEDULED → PUBLISHED → ANALYSED**

The status values used in post files (from the workbook) are:
`IDEA → SELECTED → DRAFT → AI_REVIEW → HUMAN_REVIEW → REVISION → APPROVED → SCHEDULED → PUBLISHED → ANALYSED → ARCHIVED`.
Only the founder sets `APPROVED`.

Automation may handle the repetitive stages, but human approval remains the initial publishing gate.
